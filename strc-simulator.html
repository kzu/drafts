How it works

The rules

  • Every share is bought at $100, STRC’s stated amount, and valued at $100: market prices are ignored. The initial investment buys shares at the latest close (), and the run lasts the chosen number of years from there.
  • The monthly investment buys more shares on the same day of every following month (the next weekday when it falls on a weekend).
  • Every month pays the dividend of the latest declared month (), daily. A dividend goes to the shares held before its ex-dividend date: shares bought on a day earn from the next ex-dividend date on. STRC settles in one business day, so the ex-dividend date is the record date.
  • Daily dividends, as Strategy declares them: each month pays a twelfth of the annual rate on the $100 stated amount (12% pays $1.00 a share), spread over its calendar days, weekends and holidays included, so the running total is cut to the cent and the last day takes the rest. At 12% in a 30-day month a share gets $0.03 most days and $0.04 every third day. A weekend day goes ex-dividend with the Friday before it, and each day is paid the next business day.
  • Reinvesting buys STRC at $100 on the payment day: fractional shares for the whole amount, or whole shares only, with the rest, of the dividends and of the monthly investment, waiting in cash that earns nothing until it reaches $100. Taking cash keeps the dividends out; the total adds up the position and the cash taken out. A dividend earned but not paid by the end of the run counts in the total.
  • The annualized return is the rate that, compounded daily over the time each dollar was invested, turns the money put in into what you have at the end; dividends taken as cash count on the day you get them.

Assumptions and limits

  • No taxes, fees or commissions. Strategy expects STRC’s distributions to be a return of capital for years, which lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed as income; that changes with your situation and the company’s earnings, and this page does not model it.
  • STRC trades above and below $100, so buying or selling at market prices gives a different result. Strategy adjusts the rate every month to keep it near $100.
  • The rate is not guaranteed: Strategy’s board sets it every month and may lower it, and it can suspend dividends. STRC is not backed by Strategy’s bitcoin; it has a preferred claim on what is left of the company.
  • Strategy can redeem STRC at $101 plus accrued dividends; a projection running for decades assumes it never does.
  • Weekends are the only days markets are assumed closed.

Data

The rate of the latest declared month comes from STRC’s dividends on Yahoo Finance, worked out from the daily dividends paid; earlier months are in Strategy’s 8-K filings. A GitHub workflow refreshes it every weekday after the close, so it is at most a day old. This is an illustration, not advice.
